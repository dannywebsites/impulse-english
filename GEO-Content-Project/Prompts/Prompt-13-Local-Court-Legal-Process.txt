**Personal Injury Cases in Suffolk County, MA**

Personal injury cases in Suffolk County Superior Court typically resolve in 18-24 months, though complex cases can extend longer. After filing, expect 6-8 months of discovery, followed by mandatory mediation at the courthouse on New Chardon Street. Most cases settle before trial—we've resolved 94% of our Suffolk County cases without our clients needing to testify. When trials do proceed, Judge assignments rotate, and we've appeared before every judge on the civil bench. The courthouse has limited parking; we recommend the Government Center garage.

Source: [Massachusetts Trial Court](https://www.mass.gov/orgs/trial-court)

**Divorce Process in Harris County, Texas**

Texas requires a 60-day waiting period before any divorce can be finalized, but contested divorces in Harris County Family Courts average 8-12 months. You'll file at the Harris County District Clerk on Congress Street, then attend a temporary orders hearing within 14 days if children or property disputes exist. Harris County mandates mediation before trial—we've mediated over 200 cases at the courthouse annex. Uncontested divorces with signed agreements can finalize at the 61-day mark. Courthouse security requires ID; leave pocket knives in your car.

Source: [Harris County District Clerk](https://www.cclerk.hctx.net/)

**DUI Cases in Maricopa County, Arizona**

First-offense DUI cases in Maricopa County typically resolve in 3-6 months. After arrest, you'll receive a court date for arraignment at one of the county's 26 justice courts or at Superior Court downtown if charged with felony DUI. Arizona's MVD will suspend your license 15 days after arrest unless we request a hearing within that window—a deadline many attorneys miss. We've handled 500+ DUI cases in Maricopa County courts and know which prosecutors are open to plea negotiations and which judges require ignition interlock devices even for first offenses.

Source: [Maricopa County Superior Court](https://superiorcourt.maricopa.gov/)

## SOURCE CITATIONS (MANDATORY)

**You MUST include source URLs with every legal/court process section.**

This is critical for:
1. **E-E-A-T signals** - Citing official court sources builds trust
2. **Backlink opportunities** - Links to court websites add authority
3. **Verifiability** - Court procedures can be confirmed
4. **Legal/ethical protection** - Sourced legal info is defensible

### Required Format:
```
**Source:** [Court/Agency Name](URL)
```

### Example:
```
**Source:** [Massachusetts Trial Court](https://www.mass.gov/orgs/trial-court)
```

**Preferred sources (in order):**
1. State court system websites (.gov)
2. County clerk of court websites
3. State bar association resources
4. Official court self-help portals

**If you cannot find official source:** Tell the user "I couldn't verify current court procedures for [county]. Please confirm with the clerk's office or I'll mark it as [NEEDS VERIFICATION]."

**IMPORTANT:** Never cite unofficial sources for legal procedures. Only use official court or government websites.

---

## WHAT TO AVOID

**Legal ethics violations (NEVER):**
- Guarantee specific outcomes
- Promise specific timelines as certain
- Disparage specific judges by name
- Share confidential case details

**Generic statements (NEVER USE):**
- "Every case is different" without specific timeline ranges
- "Courts are busy" without specific wait times
- "We know the local courts" without proof (case count, years)

**Missing jurisdiction specifics:**
- State-level info when county-level is needed
- Generic "courthouse" without specific location
