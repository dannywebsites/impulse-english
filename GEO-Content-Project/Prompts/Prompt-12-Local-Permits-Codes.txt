- **MAXIMUM LENGTH: 120 words** (strict limit)

2. **Permit requirement** - What work requires permits
3. **Cost/fee** - Specific permit costs if available

**Plumbing Permits in Boston**

Most plumbing work in Boston requires a permit from the Inspectional Services Department—including water heater replacements, new fixture installations, and any pipe modifications. Permit fees start at $75 for minor work, with typical approval in 3-5 business days. Only licensed plumbers can pull permits in Massachusetts. We handle all permit paperwork and schedule required inspections as part of our service.

Source: [Boston Inspectional Services Department](https://www.boston.gov/departments/inspectional-services)

**Electrical Permits in Austin, TX**

Austin requires permits for most electrical work beyond simple outlet or switch replacements. The Development Services Department charges $45-$150 depending on scope, with residential permits typically processed within 2-3 days. Texas allows homeowners to pull permits for their own residence, but all work must pass inspection. We coordinate directly with Austin inspectors and include permit costs in our quotes.

Source: [Austin Development Services](https://www.austintexas.gov/department/development-services)

### Example 3 (Roofing)

---

**Roofing Permits in Denver**

Denver requires building permits for roof replacements and repairs exceeding $5,000 in value. Permits cost $120-$300 based on project scope, processed through Denver's Development Services. Typical turnaround is 5-7 business days, though expedited review is available. Colorado's 6% sales tax applies to materials. We pull permits before starting work and ensure your roof passes final inspection—required before permit closure.

Source: [Denver Development Services](https://www.denvergov.org/Government/Agencies-Departments-Offices/Agencies-Departments-Offices-Directory/Community-Planning-and-Development)

## SOURCE CITATIONS (MANDATORY)

**You MUST include source URLs with every permits & codes section.**

This is critical for:
1. **E-E-A-T signals** - Citing official government sources builds trust
2. **Backlink opportunities** - Links to .gov sites add authority
3. **Verifiability** - Permit requirements can be confirmed
4. **Legal protection** - Sourced regulatory info is defensible

### Required Format:
```
**Source:** [City/County Department Name](URL)
```

### Example:
```
**Source:** [Boston Inspectional Services Department](https://www.boston.gov/departments/inspectional-services)
```

**Only use official sources:**
- City/County building department websites (.gov)
- State contractor licensing boards
- Official municipal code databases

**If you cannot find official source:** Tell the user "I couldn't find the official permit information for [city]. Please verify with [suggested department] or I'll mark it as [NEEDS VERIFICATION]."

## WHAT TO AVOID

**Generic statements (NEVER USE):**
- "Most cities require permits" → Name the specific city
- "Permit fees vary" → Give specific fee or range
- "Check with your local building department" → Provide the actual link

**Outdated information:**
- Always verify fees/requirements are current
- Note if recent changes occurred

**Missing official sources:**
- NEVER cite unofficial sources for permit info
- Always link to city/county government websites
