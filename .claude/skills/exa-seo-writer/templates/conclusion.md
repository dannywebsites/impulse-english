# Verdict Template (final section of every article)

> **Filename note:** this file is named `conclusion.md` for legacy reasons but the binding rule in `rules/article-types.md` is that the final section H2 must be `## Verdict` or `## Our Verdict`. Never `## Conclusion`, `## Final Thoughts`, `## Summary`, `## Key Takeaways`, or `## Wrapping Up`.

## Purpose
Pick the winner, recommend action, convert readers into clicks. The Verdict is where you earn the trust you built up across the article.

## Structure (100-250 words)

### Heading
- **Default options (pick the one that places a secondary or LSI keyword in the H2):**
  - `## Verdict on [secondary keyword]` — recommended. Example: `## Verdict on AI Search Visibility`
  - `## [secondary keyword] Verdict` — example: `## Best AI SEO Tools Verdict`
  - `## The Verdict` — when no secondary keyword fits naturally
  - `## Verdict` — only if the article is genuinely short
- **Reviews / vs:** `## Our Verdict` is fine as a starting point but prefer `## Our Verdict on [secondary keyword]` when it reads naturally
- **NEVER use:** `## Conclusion`, `## In Conclusion`, `## Final Thoughts`, `## Summary`, `## Key Takeaways`, `## Wrapping Up`, `## Your Next Steps`

The Verdict heading is your last natural opportunity to place a secondary or LSI keyword in an H2, since the title and 1–2 body H2s already carry the primary keyword. Use it deliberately.

### Paragraph 1: The Decisive Recommendation (2-3 sentences)
- State your #1 recommendation clearly
- Give the ONE reason why
- Include primary keyword

### Paragraph 2: The Segmented Recommendations (2-3 sentences)
- "If you need X, go with [Product A]."
- "If budget is your priority, [Product B] is the better fit."
- Match products to specific reader types

### Paragraph 3: The CTA (1-2 sentences)
- Tell them exactly what to do next
- For roundups: name the top pick
- For guides: encourage starting Step 1
- For informational: suggest the next action
- Do NOT insert link placeholders, image placeholders, or markdown link syntax. The CTA is plain prose — the buyer adds their own links in the CMS.

## Rules
- NO new information in the conclusion
- NO repeating the introduction
- BE DECISIVE — don't hedge with "it depends on your needs"
- Include primary keyword one final time
- Keep under 200 words — respect the reader's time
- End on a forward-looking note, not a backward summary

## CTA Formats (write as plain prose, the buyer adds links in their CMS)

- "Start with [Product]'s free plan and you'll know within a week if it fits."
- "Try [Product] free for 14 days. Upgrade only if it earns the spot."
- "See [Product]'s pricing on their site. The team plan starts under $50."
- For guides: "Start with Step 1 today. Even 15 minutes will put you ahead of 90% of people."

## Examples

### Good (Roundup):
"For most teams, Notion is the clear winner. It handles docs, tasks, and wikis in one tool without the bloat of platforms twice its price. If you're a solo user, Trello's simplicity is hard to beat. And if you need enterprise-grade reporting, Monday.com justifies the premium. Start with a free plan on any of these — you'll know within a week if it fits."

### Good (Guide):
"Starting a dropshipping store isn't complicated. It's the execution that separates the 10% who profit from the 90% who quit. Follow the steps above, start with one product in one niche, and resist the urge to scale before you have consistent sales. Your first $100 day is closer than you think."

### Bad:
"In conclusion, we have explored the various project management tools available in the market today. Each tool has its own strengths and weaknesses, and the best choice ultimately depends on your specific needs and requirements. We hope this article has been helpful in your decision-making process."

(Why it fails: starts with "In conclusion", hedges with "depends on your specific needs", no actual recommendation, uses banned words "explored" and "valuable". Violates every rule in `rules/humanizer.md`.)
