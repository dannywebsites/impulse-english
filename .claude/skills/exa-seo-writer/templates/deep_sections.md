# Deep Sections Template (Content Gap Coverage)

## Purpose
Cover topics that competitors missed. This is your competitive advantage.
The SERP research identifies "opportunity keywords" — topics with low competitor coverage.
Each gap becomes a deep section.

## When to Use
- When SERP research shows opportunity keywords
- When competitor headings analysis reveals missing topics
- When FAQ questions suggest demand for deeper coverage

## Structure Per Deep Section

```markdown
## [Opportunity Topic]

[2-3 paragraphs covering the topic thoroughly]
[Include specific data, examples, or expert perspective]
[Link to authority source if applicable]
```

### Word Count
- 200-400 words per deep section
- Don't pad — if 150 words covers it completely, that's fine
- But don't skim either — this is where you differentiate

## What Makes Deep Sections Valuable

### They answer questions nobody else is answering
- Look at the "opportunity keywords" from research
- Look at FAQ questions that competitors don't address
- Think: "What would I want to know that nobody is telling me?"

### They add genuine expertise
- Industry-specific insights
- Data that competitors don't include
- Contrarian but defensible viewpoints
- Practical advice that goes beyond surface level

### They target long-tail keywords
- Each deep section naturally targets 1-2 long-tail phrases
- These sections often rank independently for those phrases

## Selection Criteria
From the SERP research, pick deep sections based on:

1. **Opportunity keywords with highest TF-IDF score** — these are topically relevant but underused
2. **Questions from FAQ extraction that no competitor answers**
3. **Entity gaps** — tools, people, or companies competitors don't mention
4. **Subtopics from heading analysis** that only 1-2 competitors cover

## Rules
- Maximum 3 deep sections per article (don't bloat)
- Each must genuinely add value — not filler
- Include at least 1 secondary or LSI keyword per section
- These sections can be more opinionated than the main body
- Link to primary sources when making specific claims
